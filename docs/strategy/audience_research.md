## 1. Demographics

### Age & Life Stage
- **Core cohort:** 20–27 years old — post-university or in final year, actively planning migration or further study
- **Secondary cohort:** 18–19 (students preparing for undergraduate abroad) and 28–32 (working professionals seeking PR/skilled migration)
- The 20–27 segment makes up ~70% of IELTS test-takers in Nepal, India, Bangladesh, and the Philippines

### Countries & Sub-Regions
| Country | Key Cities / Regions | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Nepal | Kathmandu, Pokhara, Butwal, Biratnagar | Heavy Australia/Canada target; second-time test-takers common |
| India | Punjab, Haryana, Gujarat, Kerala, UP | Largest volume; Canada PR is top goal; Facebook-heavy audience |
| Bangladesh | Dhaka, Chittagong | UK study visa + Canada PR; mobile-only users very common |
| Philippines | Metro Manila, Cebu, Davao | UK/Canada nursing/healthcare migration; YouTube-native audience |

### Education Level
- 65%+ hold a bachelor's degree or are in the final year
- 15% diploma-holders in technical/vocational fields (nursing, engineering, hospitality)
- 20% still in undergraduate — preparing in advance for study abroad

### Income Level
- Nepal: Monthly household income NPR 30,000–80,000 (~$225–$600 USD)
- India (Punjab/Haryana): INR 25,000–60,000/month household
- Bangladesh: BDT 25,000–50,000/month
- Philippines: PHP 20,000–45,000/month
- **Key insight:** The IELTS test fee alone (USD ~$200–220) represents 30–50% of monthly income. This creates high-stakes psychology around every test attempt.

### Device Usage
- **Mobile-first, often mobile-only:** 78–85% of social video consumption on mobile (YouTube, TikTok, Facebook)
- Android dominant in Nepal, Bangladesh, India (60–75% of the market)
- iPhone usage growing in Philippines and urban India
- Home internet may be shared or unreliable — many use mobile data; prefer offline-friendly or low-data content
- Short-form video (TikTok/Reels/YouTube Shorts) is the primary discovery channel

### Language
- English is a second or third language for nearly all audience members
- They speak English at school/work but think and feel in their native language (Nepali, Hindi, Bengali, Tagalog)
- Reading English academic texts is uncomfortable — they self-describe as "not fluent enough"
- They understand English better than they speak it — listening comprehension is often better than speaking output
- IELTS is simultaneously their goal and their adversary

## 2. Psychographics

### Core Beliefs (stated and hidden)
- **"I'm not smart enough for Band 7"** — band score feels like an IQ test, not a skills test. Failure feels personal.
- "Foreign education/life is for the lucky or the rich — but maybe I can make it"
- "Hard work beats talent" — strong belief in grinding and effort. They will watch hours of content if they trust the source.
- "I wasted time in college not learning real English" — guilt and self-blame for not starting earlier
- "Coaching centers are expensive and maybe not worth it" — growing distrust of offline institutes that charge NPR 30,000+ for a course

### Identity
- **"I am a determined migrant"** — migration is the defining personal project. IELTS is the bottleneck. Every Band score point matters.
- They see themselves as the family member who is sacrificing and working hard to "go abroad" and improve the family's situation
- Failure at IELTS = failure in front of family. The shame is felt publicly — relatives ask "when are you leaving?"
- Aspirational identity: "I will be the person who got to Canada/Australia/UK/New Zealand"

### Worldview
- Education and foreign credentials = escape from local salary ceiling and limited opportunity
- Migration = dignity, stability, and a better future for their children
- English proficiency = global passport. Band 7+ feels like being accepted into a different class of person.
- They believe effort + the right system = success. They are looking for the system. Mr. Yeti can BE the system.

### Values
- Family duty over personal desire — "I'm doing this for my parents, my kids"
- Resilience — many have failed IELTS 1–3 times and are still trying
- Community trust — they rely heavily on peer reviews, YouTube comments, and Facebook group recommendations
- Frugality — they will not pay for something they don't deeply trust

## 4. Pain Points — Deep (What They Feel)

### Fear
- Fear of wasting money on test fees again (each attempt costs the equivalent of 1–3 weeks of family income)
- Fear that no matter how hard they try, they won't make it
- Fear of being stuck — watching peers leave while they're still in the waiting room
- Fear of losing the visa window — many visa categories have age cutoffs or intake deadlines

### Shame
- Shame of telling family they failed again — "How will I explain this?"
- Shame in peer groups where everyone asks "when are you going?"
- Shame of English incompetence in a society that ties English to status and intelligence

### Anxiety and Isolation
- Preparing alone at home with no accountability partner
- Feeling overwhelmed by conflicting advice (IELTS Liz says X, Simon says Y, coaching center says Z)
- Anxiety about the test environment — time pressure, strict rules, unfamiliar accent
- Feeling invisible: "No one cares if I pass or fail except my family"

### Deeper Existential Fear
- "What if I never get to Band 7?"
- "What if I'm just not good enough?"
- "My whole plan for the next 5 years depends on this test"
- The IELTS exam is not just a language test — it is THE GATE to a different life

## 5. Desires and Aspirations

### Immediate Goal (6–12 months)
- Pass IELTS with minimum Band 6.5 or 7.0
- Submit visa application (Canada Express Entry, Australia skilled migration, UK study visa, Philippine nursing overseas)
- Get that acceptance letter or visa grant

### Medium-Term Goal (2–5 years)
- Settle abroad, get permanent residency
- Find professional work in their field (nursing, engineering, IT, accounting, hospitality)
- Bring parents or spouse over
- Pay off loans taken for the move

### Lifestyle Aspiration
- "Living abroad" = safety, predictability, fair pay, rule of law
- A home they own. Healthcare. Good schools for their children.
- Being able to send money back without sacrificing their own lifestyle
- The status of "the one who made it"

### Who They Want to Become
- A confident English speaker who doesn't hesitate
- Someone respected at work — not because of their degree, but because they can communicate
- A role model for younger siblings or cousins still in the home country
- Free. Mobile. Global.

## 6. Buying Triggers

### 1. Urgency (Test Date Is Set)
- Once a test date is booked, they shift from "browsing" to "buying" mode
- The window between booking and sitting is 4–8 weeks — the highest-intent period
- Messaging: "You have 30 days. Here's what to do each day."

### 2. Quick Win (One Tip That Worked)
- If a Mr. Yeti tip helps them in real practice (e.g., the keyword technique in Reading), they trust him immediately
- Word-of-mouth within friend groups is powerful: "I tried that Mr. Yeti trick and it helped"
- Free lead magnet must deliver a genuine, observable improvement to trigger loyalty

### 3. Social Proof (Someone Like Me Got Band 7)
- Screenshots of score reports from people from their own country
- Comments like "I was stuck at 6.0 for 2 years, used pielts and got 7.5"
- Testimonials that name-drop their visa or country target ("finally got my Australia PR")

### 4. Trust Built Over Time
- They watch 10–20 videos before they will click a link
- Consistent posting (daily tips) builds compound trust
- If Mr. Yeti shows up every day and is specific and accurate, he earns authority

### 5. Price/Value Clarity
- They will pay if the value is obvious: "This is what I get, this is the cost, this is why it's worth it"
- Free trial or sample premium content lowers the first barrier
- Framing: "Less than the cost of one test retake"

### 6. FOMO / Peer Pressure
- Seeing comments like "I already signed up" or "When is this closing?"
- Limited-time offers are effective but must feel genuine

## 7. Common Objections

### "I can't afford premium"
**Reality:** They CAN afford it if they trust it. The real objection is: "I'm not sure it's worth it."  
**Counter:** Show cost vs. value — "One retake costs $200. pielts premium costs $X/month. If it helps you avoid one retake, it pays for itself 10x."

### "I have YouTube for free tips"
**Reality:** They feel scattered and overwhelmed by random YouTube content. They want structure, not more videos.  
**Counter:** "YouTube gives you tips. pielts gives you a system — a daily plan from Band 5 to Band 7 in 60 days."

### "I'm not sure if this is legit"
**Reality:** They have been burned by coaching centers that took money and delivered nothing.  
**Counter:** Show social proof prominently. Use real names and score screenshots. Mr. Yeti's cartoon face actually helps — it signals transparency and consistency, not a faceless brand trying to grab money.

### "I'll try for free first and see"
**This is the right user behavior — reward it.** Give them a genuine free experience that shows what premium looks like. Upgrade should feel natural, not forced.

### "I'll just use IELTS Liz / E2 / Simon"
**Counter:** pielts is the only free platform that gives you real practice tests with instant scoring, not just theory. Mr. Yeti is daily, visual, and made for people who learn from short content, not long lectures.

## 8. Language and Vocabulary They Use

*Direct quotes from YouTube comments, Reddit (r/IELTS), and Facebook IELTS groups:*

### Abbreviations and Shorthand
- "WT1", "WT2" = Writing Task 1, Task 2
- "GT" = General Training (visa/migration test)
- "AC" = Academic (study/university test)
- "OA" = Overall Band (e.g., "my OA is 6.5")
- "LRW S" = Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking (in that order)
- "TRF" = Test Report Form (the official score)
- "EOI" = Expression of Interest (Australian/NZ visa step)
- "EE" = Express Entry (Canada PR system)

### How They Describe Their Struggles
- "I'm stuck at 6.0 for the third time"
- "My writing always drags my overall down"
- "I blank out in speaking — I know the words but I can't speak fast enough"
- "Reading is my nemesis"
- "I always get confused with True/False/Not Given"
- "My grammar is a mess when I speak"
- "I paraphrase wrong in Task 2 intro — how to fix?"
- "Band 7 feels impossible for me"
- "Can I go from 6.0 to 7.0 in one month?"
- "I get 27–28 in Reading but need 30+"

### Motivational Phrases They Use
- "I have to do this for my family"
- "I'm not giving up"
- "One more try" / "This is my last chance"
- "Please suggest good resources"
- "Can anyone share mock tests?"
- "Praying for Band 7"

### Community Vocabulary
- "band score" not "score" or "grade"
- "mock test" not "practice test"
- "examiner" (they think a lot about what the examiner thinks)
- "template" (they desperately want writing templates)
- "strategy" (not just tips — they want a strategy)
- "improve my fluency" not "speak better English"

## 9. Content They Consume

### Top YouTube Channels (What They Watch Most)
| Channel | Why They Watch It | Weakness (Our Opportunity) |
|---|---|---|
| IELTS Liz | Comprehensive, trusted, free | Long videos, no regular short-form, no AI character |
| E2 IELTS | Great speaking content, good energy | Expensive paid tier, Australian accent can be hard |
| IELTS Simon | Clean writing templates | Very minimal, no engagement, no community |
| Ryan's IELTS | Good for prediction questions | Feels dated, less production quality |
| Asad Yaqub | Popular in South Asia | General test tips, not deep skill training |

**Mr. Yeti's opportunity:** None of these have a consistent daily short-form presence with a recognizable character. Mr. Yeti fills a gap as the "daily 60-second IELTS tip" brand.

### Platforms and When They're On
| Platform | Peak Times | Content Format |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube | 7–9pm local time, weekends | 5–15 min videos, Shorts |
| TikTok | Anytime (constant scroll), lunch breaks | 30–90 second tips |
| Instagram Reels | Evening, 8–11pm | 15–60 second Reels |
| Facebook | Morning 7–9am, evening | Posts, group discussions, videos |
| Reddit (r/IELTS) | Daytime, lunch | Text posts, score discussions |

### Facebook Groups They Join
- "IELTS Preparation — Nepal" (50k+ members)
- "IELTS Tips and Tricks" (global, 200k+ members)
- Canada/Australia visa groups where IELTS prep is a constant topic
- Country-specific WhatsApp groups for test-date sharers and mock test swaps

### What Makes Them Stop Scrolling
- Their exact problem in the thumbnail: "Stuck at IELTS 6.0?" / "Reading True/False trick"
- A cartoon character doing something relatable
- Numbers: "7 words that increase your Band score"
- Before/after: "This sentence got Band 5. This one got Band 7."
