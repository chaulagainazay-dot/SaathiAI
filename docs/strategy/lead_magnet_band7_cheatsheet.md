### SECTION 1: WRITING TASK 1 — 3 Key Rules for Band 7

**What it is:** Describe a graph, chart, map, or process (20 minutes, 150+ words)  
**What examiners assess:** Task Achievement, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy

---

**Rule 1: Describe the Trend, Not Just the Number**

Band 5 answer (wrong):
> "In 1990 the figure was 30%. In 2000 it was 45%."

Band 7 answer (right):
> "The proportion increased significantly from 30% in 1990 to 45% in 2000, representing a 15 percentage point rise over the decade."

*The examiner wants to see you interpret, not just list. Use trend language: rose sharply, declined gradually, remained stable, peaked at, fell to a low of.*

**Rule 2: Use the Overview Paragraph — It Is Non-Negotiable**

The Overview (or Summary) paragraph is the single most important paragraph for Task Achievement. Write it in 2 sentences immediately after the introduction. State the two or three most significant features without giving numbers.

Template:
> "Overall, it is clear that [main trend 1], while [main trend 2]. [Country/category X] consistently showed the highest/lowest [figure]."

Many Band 5–6 writers skip the Overview or put it at the end. Move it to paragraph 2.

**Rule 3: Group Data — Do Not Describe Line by Line**

If a bar chart has 6 countries, do NOT describe each country one sentence at a time. Group them:
- Countries with the highest figures: …
- Countries with moderate figures: …
- Countries with the lowest figures: …

This is what "Coherence and Cohesion" means at Band 7. Grouped analysis shows higher-order thinking.

### SECTION 2: WRITING TASK 2 — Band 7 Structure Template

**What it is:** Academic essay on a topic (40 minutes, 250+ words)  
**The 4 essay types:** Opinion/Argument, Discussion, Problem/Solution, Advantages/Disadvantages

---

**The Band 7 5-Paragraph Structure (works for all 4 types)**

```
PARAGRAPH 1 — INTRODUCTION (2–3 sentences)
Paraphrase the question → State your position (or say you will discuss both sides)

PARAGRAPH 2 — MAIN BODY 1 (5–7 sentences)
Topic sentence → Explain → Example → Effect/Implication

PARAGRAPH 3 — MAIN BODY 2 (5–7 sentences)
Topic sentence → Explain → Example → Effect/Implication

PARAGRAPH 4 — CONCESSION / OTHER SIDE (3–4 sentences)
(For discussion/balanced essays only)
"While some argue that…, this view overlooks…"

PARAGRAPH 5 — CONCLUSION (2–3 sentences)
Restate your position → Summarize key points → Optional forward-looking statement
```

---

**Introduction Template (copy this, adapt each time):**

> "[Paraphrased topic] has become a subject of increasing debate in recent years. While some argue that [opposing view], I firmly believe that [your position] because [brief reason]."

**Body Paragraph Template:**

> "[Topic sentence making a clear point]. This is because [explanation]. For instance, [specific real-world example]. As a result, [consequence or implication]. Therefore, [link back to the main argument]."

---

**3 Band 7 Grammar Rules for Task 2**

1. **Use complex sentences.** Every paragraph should include at least one relative clause (who/which/that), conditional (if/unless), or concession (although/despite/while).
2. **Avoid repeating the same word twice in one paragraph.** Use synonyms. (e.g., "children" → "young people", "minors", "the younger generation")
3. **Never write "In my opinion I think that…"** — it is redundant. Use one marker only: "I believe…" OR "In my view…"

### SECTION 3: READING — 3 Speed Techniques

**The IELTS Academic Reading test:** 3 passages, 40 questions, 60 minutes. Band 7 requires roughly 30–32 correct out of 40.

---

**Technique 1: Keywords First, Passage Second**

Never read the passage first. Go to the questions, underline the key content words (nouns, names, numbers, dates — NOT articles or prepositions), then scan the passage for those words.

This cuts average reading time per question from 3 minutes to 90 seconds.

Example: Question says "What year did the company expand to Europe?"  
→ Scan for: "expand", "Europe", a year (4-digit number)  
→ You will find it in seconds without reading 3 paragraphs.

**Technique 2: True / False / Not Given — The One Rule That Fixes It**

This is the most failed question type. The rule:
- **TRUE** = the passage DIRECTLY confirms the statement (same meaning)
- **FALSE** = the passage DIRECTLY contradicts the statement (opposite meaning)
- **NOT GIVEN** = the passage does not say it either way — no information at all

The mistake: Students choose TRUE when the information is "probably true" or "makes sense." If it is not explicitly stated in the text, it is NOT GIVEN.

Practice test: Read the statement. Find the relevant sentence in the passage. Ask: does this sentence confirm, contradict, or ignore the statement? Choose accordingly.

**Technique 3: Matching Headings — Eliminate, Don't Select**

For Matching Headings questions, DO NOT try to find the "best" heading for each paragraph. Instead:
1. Read the paragraph opening and closing sentence (not the whole thing)
2. Eliminate headings that are clearly wrong
3. Choose from the remaining 2–3 options

This uses elimination logic, which is faster and more accurate than positive selection.

### SECTION 4: LISTENING — 3 Prediction Tricks

**The IELTS Listening test:** 4 sections, 40 questions, ~30 minutes of audio. Band 7 requires roughly 30 correct.

---

**Trick 1: Use the 30-Second Gap to Predict**

Before each section begins, you have 30 seconds to read the questions. Use this time to:
- Identify what type of information is missing (a name? a number? a date? a reason?)
- Predict what word class you need (noun? adjective? verb?)
- Circle any proper nouns so you listen for them specifically

This transforms you from a passive listener into an active hunter.

**Trick 2: Listen for Signpost Words**

Speakers often use signpost words before the answer:
- "The reason is…" → your answer follows
- "The main problem is…" → your answer follows
- "However…" / "But actually…" → previous information was wrong; this is the correction
- "So that means…" → implication = likely your answer

Train your ear for these signals in mock tests.

**Trick 3: Expect Distractors — The First Answer Is Often Wrong**

In IELTS Listening, speakers often self-correct or change their answer:
- "We'll meet on Tuesday — actually, let's make it Wednesday."
- "The price is $45… wait, with the discount it's $38."

The final stated answer is correct. If you mark the first number/day/name you hear, you will lose marks. Always listen until the speaker finishes their complete thought.

### SECTION 5: SPEAKING — 10 Band 7 Vocabulary Phrases

**The IELTS Speaking test:** 3 parts, 11–14 minutes. Band 7 requires fluency, coherence, vocabulary range, and grammatical accuracy.

---

**Why Vocabulary Matters for Band 7**

Band 5–6 vocabulary is basic and repetitive: "good", "bad", "important", "many people think".  
Band 7 vocabulary is precise, idiomatic, and natural.

**10 Phrases to Replace Common Band 5–6 Words:**

| Band 5–6 (Avoid) | Band 7 Replacement | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| "It is very important" | "It plays a crucial role in…" | "Technology plays a crucial role in education today." |
| "Many people think" | "There is a growing consensus that…" | "There is a growing consensus that remote work benefits productivity." |
| "A lot of" | "A significant proportion of…" | "A significant proportion of young people prefer streaming to TV." |
| "Good for the environment" | "Environmentally sustainable / eco-friendly" | "Electric vehicles are far more environmentally sustainable." |
| "I think" (repeated) | "From my perspective…" / "In my experience…" | "From my perspective, family support is irreplaceable." |
| "Bad effect" | "detrimental impact on…" | "Pollution has a detrimental impact on public health." |
| "Because of this" | "As a consequence…" / "This has led to…" | "As a consequence, many families have relocated." |
| "Very fast" | "at an unprecedented rate" | "Technology is advancing at an unprecedented rate." |
| "Help people" | "empower individuals to…" | "Online learning empowers individuals to study at their own pace." |
| "I don't know" (when stuck) | "That's an interesting question. Let me think about that for a moment…" | Buys thinking time without losing fluency marks. |

**Fluency Rule:** Use the last phrase — "That's an interesting question…" — anytime you need 3–5 seconds to think. Examiners reward it as a natural speaking strategy, not as hesitation.

### SECTION 6: Common Mistakes at Band 5–6 That Block Band 7

**Writing Mistakes**
- ❌ Copying words from the question directly into the introduction without paraphrasing
- ❌ Writing the overview at the end of Task 1 instead of paragraph 2
- ❌ Using general examples ("For example, in many countries...") instead of specific ones ("For example, in Germany, renewable energy now supplies 40% of electricity needs.")
- ❌ Using "firstly, secondly, thirdly, finally" as the ONLY cohesive devices
- ❌ Writing fewer than 250 words in Task 2 (automatic penalty)

**Speaking Mistakes**
- ❌ Giving one-sentence answers — always extend with "because", "for example", "which means that"
- ❌ Self-correcting too often — practice saying the next best word, not the perfect word
- ❌ Memorized answers that sound rehearsed — examiners are trained to detect and penalize these
- ❌ Ignoring Part 2 timing — practice speaking for exactly 1 minute 45 seconds

**Reading Mistakes**
- ❌ Reading the whole passage before looking at questions — always questions first
- ❌ Spending more than 90 seconds per question — move on and return if time allows
- ❌ Changing a correct answer to an incorrect one (first instinct is usually right for experienced test-takers)

**Listening Mistakes**
- ❌ Spelling errors — listen for British spelling ("colour", "organisation")
- ❌ Missing plural/singular — "book" vs "books" counts as wrong
- ❌ Not transferring answers carefully in the transfer time — read what you wrote out loud mentally
