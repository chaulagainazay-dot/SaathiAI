## Persona 1: Struggling Sita

**"I've failed twice. I can't afford a third attempt."**

### Demographics
- **Name:** Sita Thapa (representative Nepali name)
- **Age:** 22
- **Location:** Pokhara or Butwal, Nepal (mid-sized city, not Kathmandu)
- **Occupation:** Nursing graduate, waiting to work abroad
- **Income/Budget:** Personal savings NPR 10,000–20,000 for exam prep; can't afford a coaching center
- **Device:** Android smartphone (mid-range), occasional laptop at home
- **Internet:** Mobile data, sometimes Wi-Fi at home; data-conscious

### IELTS Goal
- **Target band:** 6.5 overall, minimum 6.5 in each module
- **Purpose:** UK nursing registration (NMC requires 6.5 in all modules)
- **Urgency:** High — has already failed twice (Band 6.0 overall, 5.5 in Writing)
- **Timeline:** Must pass in next 6 months or reconsider plans

### Pain Points
- Failed specifically on Writing — doesn't know why her score is 5.5
- Coaching centers cost NPR 25,000+, which she's already spent once
- Free YouTube content is scattered, inconsistent, and often not explained clearly
- She knows her English is decent but IELTS format trips her up
- Family pressure is immense — parents sacrificed for her nursing degree expecting UK income
- Afraid of trying again and failing a third time (emotional block)

### Goals
- Understand exactly what Band 6.5 Writing looks like
- Get daily structured tips without paying
- Practice writing tasks and get a sense of whether she's improving
- Feel less alone in her IELTS struggle

### Online Behavior
- **YouTube:** Watches IELTS tip videos daily, subscribes to multiple channels, watches 3–8 videos per study session
- **Facebook:** Active in Nepali IELTS Facebook groups (shares tips, asks questions)
- **TikTok:** Uses it for entertainment, but will engage with IELTS tips if they appear
- **Instagram:** Uses for personal content; less likely to seek IELTS content here
- **Google:** Searches "IELTS Writing Task 2 tips", "how to get Band 6.5 writing", "free IELTS practice"

### Content She Consumes
- Short specific tips (not general "how to study" advice)
- Before/after essay examples ("This essay is Band 5.5 — here's why. This one is Band 6.5 — see the difference")
- Nepali-language or bilingual content when confused
- Emotional/motivational content from people who've failed and recovered

### What Makes Her Buy (Premium)
- She sees consistent improvement in practice tests on pielts.web.app
- A specific feature addresses her Writing weakness (e.g., AI writing feedback)
- The price is NPR 500–800/month (≈ $3.80–$6.00 USD) — within solo savings
- She's in the final 4 weeks before her exam and needs intensive practice
- A free trial or money-back promise reduces risk

### Mr. Yeti Content That Resonates
- "Why your IELTS Writing is stuck at Band 5.5 (and one fix)"
- "3 Band 6.5 Writing phrases examiners love"
- "I failed IELTS. Here's what I changed."

## Persona 2: Ambitious Arjun

**"I need Band 7.5. I've done the math. I need Canada."**

### Demographics
- **Name:** Arjun Sharma (representative Indian name)
- **Age:** 26
- **Location:** Bangalore or Pune, India
- **Occupation:** Software engineer (2–3 years experience), IT services company
- **Income/Budget:** INR 40,000–60,000/month; can spend INR 3,000–5,000 on test prep without stress
- **Device:** iPhone or high-end Android; laptop; good home Wi-Fi
- **Internet:** No data limitations; streams freely

### IELTS Goal
- **Target band:** 7.5 overall (7.0+ in each module)
- **Purpose:** Canada Express Entry PR — IELTS score affects CRS points directly
- **Urgency:** Medium — planning to apply in 6–12 months, wants to optimize CRS score
- **Timeline:** Likely to take IELTS once, maybe twice; will pay for coaching if needed

### Pain Points
- His English is strong professionally, but IELTS speaking and writing formats are different from workplace English
- He's researched the exam extensively and wants Band 7.5+ specifically, not just "pass"
- Generic IELTS content is too basic for him — needs advanced tips (Band 7 → Band 8 strategies)
- Time-poor: works 9–6, studies evenings and weekends; doesn't want long video courses
- Doesn't want to pay INR 30,000 for offline coaching when he can learn faster online

### Goals
- Understand what separates Band 7 from Band 8 in Speaking and Writing
- Practice efficiently — doesn't have 4 hours/day to study
- Get accurate mock test scores to track progress
- Pass in one attempt (failure has professional embarrassment implications)

### Online Behavior
- **YouTube:** Watches Band 7+ specific content; searches "IELTS Band 8 speaking tips", "difference between Band 7 and 7.5"
- **Reddit:** Active on r/IELTSPrep and r/ImmigrationCanada
- **LinkedIn:** Passive, not an IELTS content space for him
- **Instagram/TikTok:** Scrolls for entertainment; not his primary IELTS research channel but will save relevant content
- **Google:** Extensive research; reads blog posts, comparisons

### Content He Consumes
- Advanced tips and nuance ("not just avoid contractions — here's when native speakers break this rule")
- Data-backed content ("Band 7 essays use linking devices 8–12 times per 250 words")
- Comparison content (Band 6 vs Band 7 vs Band 8 examples)
- Efficient study plans ("Prepare for IELTS in 3 weeks if your English is already strong")

### What Makes Him Buy (Premium)
- Platform offers realistic mock tests with accurate band score estimation
- AI feedback on his writing with specific Band 7+ improvement notes
- He can see his score improvement over practice attempts
- Monthly price is under INR 400 (≈ $4.80 USD) — trivial relative to his exam cost
- He finds the free tier genuinely useful and trusts the brand before upgrading

### Mr. Yeti Content That Resonates
- "Band 7 Speaking: The difference between 'fluent' and 'Band 8'"

- "IELTS for IT professionals: what's different"

## Persona 3: Busy Bina

**"I study between night shifts. I need Band 7 for Australia."**

### Demographics
- **Name:** Bina Santos (representative Filipino name)
- **Age:** 28
- **Location:** Manila or Cebu, Philippines
- **Occupation:** Registered nurse, working at a public hospital (night and day shifts)
- **Income/Budget:** PHP 25,000–35,000/month; can spend PHP 500–1,500/month on prep tools
- **Device:** Android phone (primary); no reliable laptop; studies entirely on mobile
- **Internet:** Home Wi-Fi or hospital cafeteria Wi-Fi during breaks

### IELTS Goal
- **Target band:** 7.0 overall (minimum 7.0 in Speaking and Listening, 6.5 in Reading/Writing)
- **Purpose:** Australian nursing registration (AHPRA + immigration requirement)
- **Urgency:** Very high — her agency has submitted her application, exam date is in 3 months
- **Timeline:** One attempt is budgeted; second attempt means more delay and agency fees

### Pain Points
- Studies in 20–40 minute windows between shifts — cannot do long study sessions
- Is already a competent English speaker (works in an English-medium hospital) but exam format is unfamiliar
- Anxious about IELTS Academic vs. General Training differences — she needs Academic
- Finds most YouTube content too long; wants quick wins she can apply immediately
- The stress of the exam overlaps with shift fatigue — motivation drops easily

### Goals
- Get quick, practitioner-applicable tips that don't require hours of study
- Understand exactly what IELTS Academic Reading and Listening expect vs. her daily English
- Practice during breaks — needs a mobile-first, low-data-friendly platform
- Feel confident about her Speaking band (her biggest concern is accent bias)

### Online Behavior
- **Facebook:** Primary platform — Filipino IELTS prep groups, nursing OFW communities
- **YouTube:** Watches on phone before sleep or during commute; prefers Shorts over long-form
- **TikTok:** Uses it actively; IELTS Shorts catch her attention while scrolling
- **Messenger:** Group chats with colleagues preparing for the same exam
- **Google:** Searches "IELTS tips for nurses", "IELTS Academic or General for Australia nursing"

### Content She Consumes
- Bite-sized tips she can apply immediately
- Content addressing accent/pronunciation concerns ("your Filipino accent won't fail you — here's what actually matters")
- Motivational content from fellow Filipino nurses who passed
- Platform content she can use during a 20-minute break (short tests, flashcards)

### What Makes Her Buy (Premium)
- Mobile-first platform that works during short breaks
- Listening practice tests (her #1 practice need — hard to replicate without a platform)
- Price at PHP 200–400/month (≈ $3.50–$7.00 USD) — affordable on her budget
- A Facebook group or community element (she's community-oriented)
- She's already using the free tier and trusts Mr. Yeti

### Mr. Yeti Content That Resonates
- "IELTS Listening for nurses: the 4 section types explained in 60 seconds"
- "Your accent won't fail IELTS Speaking. This will."
- "How to study for IELTS between shifts (realistic plan)"

## Persona 4: First-timer Farhan

**"I don't even know where to start. Everyone says IELTS is hard."**

### Demographics
- **Name:** Farhan Ahmed (representative Bangladeshi name)
- **Age:** 20
- **Location:** Dhaka or Chittagong, Bangladesh
- **Occupation:** Undergraduate student (3rd year), engineering or business
- **Income/Budget:** Near-zero personal budget; depends on family; free tools only for now
- **Device:** Mid-range Android; unreliable mobile data; slow home broadband
- **Internet:** Data-sensitive; avoids high-bandwidth videos when not on Wi-Fi

### IELTS Goal
- **Target band:** 6.0–6.5 overall
- **Purpose:** UK or Canada undergraduate/postgraduate admission; first step toward going abroad
- **Urgency:** Low-medium — exam is 4–6 months away; hasn't registered yet
- **Timeline:** First attempt; wants to pass without repeating

### Pain Points
- Completely overwhelmed — doesn't know the difference between Academic and General Training
- Unsure of his current English level or what band he'd get today
- IELTS resources online are vast and contradictory — doesn't know what to trust
- Family doesn't understand why YouTube can't just "teach" him IELTS for free
- Afraid of the cost of the exam itself (BDT 16,000+) — doesn't want to waste a sitting
- Comparing himself to classmates who are already preparing with coaching centers

### Goals
- Understand the IELTS exam structure clearly (what each module is, how it's scored)
- Know his current approximate band level before spending money on an exam
- Get a simple study plan he can follow without confusion
- Find a free practice platform that doesn't require credit card or signup hassle

### Online Behavior
- **YouTube:** Heavy user; searches "IELTS for beginners", "IELTS Band 6 tips", "what is IELTS"
- **Facebook:** In Bangladeshi study-abroad and IELTS groups; reads comments more than he posts
- **TikTok:** Active user; short IELTS tips catch his attention between entertainment content
- **WhatsApp:** Group chats with classmates studying for the same exam
- **Google:** Searches "how to prepare for IELTS at home free", "IELTS practice test free Bangladesh"

### Content He Consumes
- Beginner explainers ("IELTS explained in 5 minutes")
- Full exam walkthroughs (what happens in each module)
- Study plan templates ("90-day IELTS study plan for beginners")
- Testimonials from students who passed with self-study
- Platform content that feels safe and non-judgmental for beginners

### What Makes Him Buy (Premium)
- He won't buy in month 1 — he's a long free-tier user
- He converts when he's 4–6 weeks from a scheduled exam date and needs structured mock tests
- Price must be BDT 200–300/month (≈ $1.80–$2.70 USD) — the lowest feasible price point
- A one-time "exam prep pack" offer may outperform a subscription for this persona
- He buys when he trusts the brand completely — trust is built over 2–3 months of free content

### Mr. Yeti Content That Resonates
- "What is IELTS? Everything a beginner needs to know (3 minutes)"
- "IELTS Academic vs. General Training: which one do you need?"
- "Free 30-day IELTS study plan — start here"
- "What Band 6 actually looks like (real examples)"
