## Email 3 — Day 4
**Subject line:** How to practice Task 2 writing in 20 minutes a day
**Preview text:** No, you don't need to write a full essay every day

---

Hi [First Name],

Good news: you do NOT need to write a full 250-word essay every single day.

That's exhausting, and most students burn out by Week 2.

Here's the 20-minute daily routine that actually works:

**Minutes 0–5: Read the prompt.**
Underline the key words. Ask: "Is this Opinion? Discussion? Problem-Solution?" Identify the type before you write a single word.

**Minutes 5–10: Outline only.**
Write 4 bullet points — one per paragraph. Don't write full sentences. Just your main idea for each paragraph.

**Minutes 10–18: Write ONE paragraph.**
Pick the paragraph you find hardest (usually the body paragraphs). Write it in full. Focus on one thing: does every sentence connect back to your main idea?

**Minutes 18–20: Review.**
Read it out loud. Does it flow? Did you use a linking phrase? Did you give an example?

That's it. 20 minutes. One paragraph per day.

Do this 5 days a week for 4 weeks and you'll have practiced 20 body paragraphs. That's more structured practice than most Band 7 students do in 3 months.

**Preview text:** Plus: your free practice resource (you haven't tried this yet)

---

Hi [First Name],

Let's talk about Speaking — the section most students dread but where your score can improve *fastest*.

**Tip 1: Buy yourself 2 seconds with a starter phrase.**
When Part 3 questions feel too sudden, use: "That's an interesting question. I'd say..." or "Hmm, I haven't really thought about that before, but I suppose..."
This isn't cheating — examiners expect thinking time. It sounds natural, not rehearsed.

**Tip 2: Extend every answer with "because" and "for example."**
Short answers kill your score. If you say "I prefer cities," add "...because I enjoy the convenience of public transport. For example, in Kathmandu, I can get anywhere without a car."
Two words. Double the length. Better score.

**Tip 3: Don't correct yourself mid-sentence.**
Most students say "I was... I mean, I am..." when they make a mistake. Fluent speakers often just *continue.* Unless it changes the meaning, keep going.
