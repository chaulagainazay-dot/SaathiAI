# pielts Hook Library — 50 Viral Video Hooks
**Brand:** pielts x Mr. Yeti
**Format:** Each hook is under 10 words for use as a video opening line or on-screen text

---

## CATEGORY 1: Fear / Warning Hooks (10)
*Pattern: Stop [doing X] / You're losing points because...*

---

**F1.**
> "Stop saying 'however' in every IELTS essay."
- Topic: Writing
- Format: Statement
- Why it works: Super-specific. Every student is guilty of this. Immediate self-recognition.

---

**F2.**
> "This one word is costing you a band score."
- Topic: Writing / General
- Format: Shocking fact (implied)
- Why it works: Mystery + loss aversion. Viewer must watch to find out the word.

---

**F3.**
> "You're practising IELTS the wrong way. Here's proof."
- Topic: General
- Format: Shocking statement
- Why it works: Challenges existing behaviour. "Here's proof" signals evidence, not opinion.

---

**F4.**
> "Stop memorising vocabulary lists for IELTS."
- Topic: General / Vocabulary
- Format: Contrarian command
- Why it works: Goes against what every student is doing. Instant scroll-stopper.

---

**F5.**
> "This Speaking mistake drops you to Band 5 instantly."
- Topic: Speaking
- Format: Warning / consequence
- Why it works: Band score specificity makes the threat concrete and believable.

---

**F6.**
> "Your IELTS Reading strategy is backwards."
- Topic: Reading
- Format: Shocking statement
- Why it works: "Backwards" is unexpected. Forces the viewer to question their entire method.

---

**F7.**
> "Warning: this Listening habit makes you miss 10 answers."
- Topic: Listening
- Format: Warning
- Why it works: "10 answers" quantifies the damage. Specific number = credibility.

---

**F8.**
> "Don't write this sentence in IELTS Task 2. Ever."
- Topic: Writing
- Format: Command / warning
- Why it works: "Ever" is absolute. Viewer is desperate to know what the sentence is.

---

**F9.**
> "You're losing Speaking marks before you open your mouth."
- Topic: Speaking
- Format: Shocking statement
- Why it works: Paradox. How do you lose marks before speaking? Must watch to find out.

---

**F10.**
> "IELTS examiners are trained to catch this mistake."
- Topic: General
- Format: Insider reveal
- Why it works: Positions examiner as adversary. Triggers paranoia + curiosity.

## CATEGORY 2: Curiosity Hooks (10)
*Pattern: The secret X / What examiners won't tell you...*

---

**C1.**
> "The IELTS secret examiners won't tell you."
- Topic: General
- Format: Insider secret
- Why it works: Classic "forbidden knowledge" frame. Viewer assumes the examiner is hiding something.

---

**C2.**
> "Why Band 7 students write shorter essays than Band 5."
- Topic: Writing
- Format: Counterintuitive fact
- Why it works: Reverses the assumption that longer = better. Immediately surprising.

---

**C3.**
> "IELTS has a hidden scoring pattern. Here it is."
- Topic: General
- Format: Insider reveal
- Why it works: "Hidden pattern" triggers curiosity. "Here it is" gives instant payoff promise.

---

**C4.**
> "What separates Band 6 from Band 7 in 10 seconds."
- Topic: General
- Format: Contrast / intrigue
- Why it works: Specific time frame ("10 seconds") makes the gap feel identifiable, not overwhelming.

---

**C5.**
> "Nobody talks about this IELTS Speaking trick."
- Topic: Speaking
- Format: Gap in common knowledge
- Why it works: Social proof in reverse — if nobody talks about it, it must be secret value.

---

**C6.**
> "This Reading technique is used by Band 9 students only."
- Topic: Reading
- Format: Exclusivity / insider knowledge
- Why it works: "Band 9 only" creates a velvet rope. Viewer wants in.

---

**C7.**
> "The real reason you fail IELTS Writing — not grammar."
- Topic: Writing
- Format: Surprising revelation
- Why it works: Dismisses the common belief (grammar = everything). Viewer needs to know the real reason.

---

**C8.**
> "IELTS asks the same 4 question types. Always."
- Topic: Writing / Reading
- Format: Pattern reveal
- Why it works: "Always" is absolute and reassuring. If it's always the same 4, prep becomes manageable.

---

**C9.**
> "The one sentence structure that impresses every examiner."
- Topic: Writing / Speaking
- Format: Silver bullet
- Why it works: One = achievable. Examiner = authority. Viewer wants the single thing that works.

---

**C10.**
> "Your dictionary is lying to you about IELTS vocabulary."
- Topic: Vocabulary / General
- Format: Shocking accusation
- Why it works: Personifies the dictionary as deceptive. Goes against a trusted tool. Disruptive.

---

**R1.**
> "How students go from Band 5 to Band 7 in 30 days."

- Format: Transformation story

---

**R2.**
> "She got Band 7 Writing after changing one thing."

- Format: Story-driven result
- Why it works: "One thing" is low-effort. Story framing (she/he) adds authenticity.

---

**R3.**

## CATEGORY 5: Question Hooks (10)
*Pattern: Can you answer this? / What would you do?*

---

**Q1.**
> "Can you answer this IELTS Task 2 question correctly?"
- Topic: Writing
- Format: Challenge question
- Why it works: Puts the viewer on the spot. Ego and curiosity drive the watch.

---

**Q2.**
> "What's the difference between 'although' and 'however'?"
- Topic: Writing / Grammar
- Format: Knowledge test
- Why it works: Deceptively simple. Most students think they know but aren't sure. Comments will explode.

---

**Q3.**
> "Which answer is correct — A, B, or C? Most get this wrong."
- Topic: Listening / Reading
- Format: Quiz challenge
- Why it works: "Most get this wrong" triggers competitive instinct. Quiz format = high engagement.

---

**Q4.**
> "Can you spot the Band 5 mistake in this essay?"
- Topic: Writing
- Format: Error-spotting challenge
- Why it works: Interactive + educational. Comments become self-generated answers, boosting reach.

---

**Q5.**
> "What band score would this Speaking answer get?"
- Topic: Speaking
- Format: Scoring challenge
- Why it works: Viewer becomes the examiner. Empowers them. Comments argue over the score.

---

**Q6.**
> "Is this sentence too informal for IELTS Writing Task 1?"
- Topic: Writing
- Format: Judgment question
- Why it works: Task 1 formality is a real grey area. Generates genuine debate.

---

**Q7.**
> "How many answers did you get right in 60 seconds?"
- Topic: Reading / Listening
- Format: Timed challenge
- Why it works: Speed + score = competitive pressure. Works extremely well as a Reel/Short format.

---

**Q8.**
> "Can you complete this sentence the Band 7 way?"
- Topic: Writing / Speaking
- Format: Completion challenge
- Why it works: "Band 7 way" creates aspiration. Fill-in-the-blank = very low barrier to comment.

---

**Q9.**
> "Which of these words is wrong in IELTS? Guess first."
- Topic: Vocabulary
- Format: Guessing challenge
- Why it works: "Guess first" forces commitment before the answer reveals. Increases video watch time.

---

**Q10.**
> "Would you pass IELTS Listening with this audio clip?"
- Topic: Listening
- Format: Self-assessment challenge
- Why it works: Vulnerability test. Students are anxious about Listening — testing themselves safely = comfort.
